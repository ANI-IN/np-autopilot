A knowledge graph over Interview Kickstart’s New Programs corpus — domains, programs, modules, instructors, and the workflows that run them.

Counts here are floors and a miss is not evidence of absence — what that means is below.

Sign in with your Interview Kickstart Workspace account.

What it answers well

Who staffs a domain. Owners and delivery, and the instructors who teach its modules.
What a workflow involves. Its steps, effort, tools and alert conditions.
Where a fact came from. Every node cites the file, sheet and row it was read from — and says when a person entered it by hand instead.

What it will not tell you

A miss is not evidence of absence. Search matches labels in this graph. Not finding something means this graph does not hold it, which is not the same as the corpus not holding it.
Counts are floors, not totals. Not every worksheet in the corpus has been read by an extraction scan, so people, instructors and modules are lower bounds. Treat them as “at least”.
Some emptiness is the right answer. Workflows have no owners because ownership at New Programs attaches to domains, not workflows. Android and iOS have no instructors. Both are correct, not gaps.
It refuses rather than guesses. Where the evidence is genuinely ambiguous — three different people called Karthika, an alias that could mean two domains — it says so instead of picking one.
